# Seedance 2.5 Couple Start Benchmark

## Status

`DOCUMENTED FROM REAL USER GENERATIONS`

Two 5-second Seedance 2.5 couple-start videos were generated from the validated lean-in / almost-kiss setup.

---

## Version A — Standard

Observed:

- stronger overall romantic tension despite being labeled the less-intimate version;
- directional side/back light gave faces more separation, contour and readable skin / hair shape;
- approach remained unresolved long enough to preserve anticipation;
- the final state retained a small remaining distance rather than collapsing immediately into contact;
- motion read as more controlled and emotionally charged.

Decision:

`PASS — preferred current Seedance start behavior`

---

## Version B — More Intimate

Observed:

- more literal progression toward physical contact;
- brighter, flatter white backlight reduced facial light/shadow separation;
- higher-key background made the image feel less dimensional than Version A;
- because the approach completed more directly, the sequence felt less ambiguous / less teasing even though the prompt requested more intimacy.

Decision:

`PASS WITH LEARNING — useful counterexample`

---

## Distilled Rule

Current test supports:

`MORE INTIMATE WORDING ≠ MORE ROMANTIC TENSION`

For this couple / setup, tension improved when the video used:

- slow physical approach;
- stable gaze;
- controlled hand placement;
- a micro-pause;
- unresolved face distance;
- dimensional side/back light rather than flat white backlight.

Do not generalize lighting taste universally, but preserve the motion principle:

`APPROACH → HOLD → DO NOT RESOLVE TOO EARLY`

---

## Identity / Motion Notes

The tested short-start approach maintained both subjects sufficiently for continued development. However, longer multi-shot Seedance continuity has not yet been validated.

Future tests should separately validate:

- longer duration;
- automatic cuts;
- different scenes / wardrobe colors;
- continuity after a first intimacy beat;
- identity under larger head / body motion.
